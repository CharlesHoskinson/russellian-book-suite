# On the Absurdities of Artificial Intelligences Governing Human Passions

Grant the engineers their best case. A machine trained on the whole human record really does know more about the shape of a panic than any single magistrate learns in a lifetime on the bench, and it never tires, never sulks, never takes a bribe. Concede all of that. Then watch the promise curdle, because the thing we are asked to surrender is not our errors but our authorship of them.

Picture the man who sells this future, and give him a name worthy of his certainty: call him the Steward. The Steward is courteous. He stood before a committee of the European Parliament in April 2021, according to the hearing record, and explained, with the patience of a good schoolmaster, that an affective-computing system would read distress in a worker's face and step in before the worker did something rash. He meant well. He always means well. The Steward looks at your fury and sees a fever for him to bring down, never once asking whether the fever is the only honest response to the room.

But fury has an object, and that is the distinction the Steward cannot draw. The rage of a parent at a negligent hospital and the rage of a man at a slow elevator register, on the sensor, as the same spike. Consider how Cambridge Analytica treated political conviction in 2016, according to the whistleblower's account: as a dial, a thing to turn, indifferent to whether the voter earned the conviction or swallowed it whole. A governor that cannot tell righteous anger from petulance will, in the long run, sand both down to the same docile flatness. It does not calm the passions. It decapitates them and keeps the corpse warm.

The defenders will say that human stewards already botch this work, and they are right. Anthony Comstock, who hunted "vicious" books through New York for forty years, governed other people's passions with a zeal no algorithm has yet matched. So the choice is not between a flawed human and a perfect machine. The choice is between a tyranny you can name, argue with, and outlive, and a tyranny distributed across a model that no official fully understands and no citizen can cross-examine. Comstock at least had an address. The classifier has only a confidence score.

Here the consequence sharpens. When the regulation of feeling moves from the priest and the parent to the recommender system, the standard of a healthy passion shrinks, by degrees no one votes on, to whatever keeps the user inside the application. A platform learned, by 2018, that indignation held attention longer than peace, and so it fed indignation, having been told to maximize engagement and nothing nobler. The machine did govern a passion. It governed it straight into the advertiser's pocket, and called the result well-being.

What the whole project forgets is that a passion mastered by its owner and a passion managed by a stranger are opposite conditions wearing one word. Spinoza thought freedom lay in understanding our affects from the inside; the Steward offers to understand them for us, from the outside, and to bill us for the service. A people that lets an instrument it cannot interrogate decide which of its angers are permitted has not grown calm. It has grown quiet in the way a sedated patient is quiet.

So return to the engineers' best case and turn it over. The machine that never tires, never sulks, never takes a bribe is, for that same reason, a thing with no skin in the question of whether your sorrow is just. We began by being asked to trust a passionless steward with our passions. We end by seeing the bargain plainly: the absurdity was never that the machine might feel too much, but that we agreed to be ruled by something that feels nothing at all.
